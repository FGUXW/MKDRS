## The beginning

When Malcolm and Maureen Pruskin moved to Milton Keynes in 1977, they found no established Jewish community. Maureen's mother, Kitty Morris, placed a letter in the Jewish Chronicle inviting interested local people to get in touch.

The response led to the first service on 4 March 1978. Rabbi Larry Tabick led the service, with representatives from the Reform Synagogues of Great Britain among the guests. Milton Keynes Reform Synagogue was established soon afterwards.

## The early years

Services and social events first took place in members' homes. As the community grew, it began hiring local halls. The name later changed to Milton Keynes and District Reform Synagogue, reflecting membership from places including Bedford, Leighton Buzzard and Northampton.

From 1988, the community had priority use of the small meeting place at Tinkers Bridge. Children's education classes were held at the Open University. A small satellite community was also established in Kettering during this period.

Members became increasingly involved in interfaith work. MKDRS continues to contribute to the wider faith and community life of Milton Keynes.

## A permanent home

Rabbi William Wolff became the community rabbi in November 1990. After he moved to Brighton three years later, the congregation focused on raising funds for a permanent synagogue building.

Work on the synagogue building started on 15 May 2002. The first service in the new synagogue took place on 17 August 2002, when Jonathan Dryer celebrated his Bar Mitzvah. The building was formally dedicated on 6 April 2003.

## An established community shul

In 2006, MKDRS appointed Rabbi Shulamit Ambalu, who served the community for ten years.

Our first open day in 2018 welcomed about 200 visitors of all faiths and none. We held another successful open day in 2023 as part of Heritage Open Days.

In July 2026, Rabbi Yael Tischler started work with MKDRS as a freelance rabbi.
