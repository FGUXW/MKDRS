## Jerusalem scroll

Our Jerusalem scroll is our youngest Torah scroll and the one we use most often.

A full photographic record of the scroll is available by book: Genesis, Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers and Deuteronomy.

Duncan Bennett photographed the scroll with help from Stan Cohen.

## Pacov scroll

We are proud to care for Czech Memorial Scroll number 970. It comes from Pacov in Czechia and is believed to be about 250 years old.

During the Second World War, 1,564 Torah scrolls from Jewish communities across Bohemia and Moravia were brought to Prague. The scrolls were later acquired by Westminster Synagogue in London. The Czech Memorial Scrolls Trust now cares for them and lends them to Jewish communities around the world.

Pacov once had a thriving Jewish community. In November 1942, 97 Jewish residents were deported to Theresienstadt and later to Auschwitz. Only six survived.

In 2017, local residents established Tikkun Pacov to keep alive the Jewish history and cultural heritage in the Pacov area. In 2008, they bought the old synagogue and are in the process of restoring and converting it into a museum and memorial. In November 2022, MKDRS members travelled to Pacov for events marking 80 years since the deportation of its Jewish community.

## Iraqi scroll

Our third scroll is believed to be Iraqi in origin and between 150 and 200 years old. It was originally held in a tik, a rigid case, and was later mounted on atzei chayim, or wooden rollers. The scroll was bought from the Brighton New Synagogue in 1992.
